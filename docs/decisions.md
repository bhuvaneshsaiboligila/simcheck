| 34 | 2026-11-05 | **Correction to decision #32**: non-orthogonality DOES need inverted-cell handling after all — #32's reasoning (bounded `acos`, no volume term) proved boundedness but not correctness, and Bhuvanesh caught the actual bug: `cylinder_3d.msh`'s non-orthogonality showed a plausible-looking 99–177° range, but that range itself was wrong, not just absent-sentinel. A face's area vector `S` is taken from its owner's winding (and, for an internal face, the same `S` is shared with the neighbour); if the owner — or neighbour — is inverted, that cell's flipped corner order flips `S`'s sign, which flips `dot(d,S)/(\|d\|\|S\|)` and reports close to the COMPLEMENTARY angle (180°−true), not the true one — worse than a sentinel, since it looks like a real number. Fix: `nonOrthogonalityFindings` now excludes any FACE where the owner or neighbour has volume ≤ 0 from the per-cell max-reduction, tier counts, histogram, min/max/mean, and worst-cell list; a cell with at least one clean face is still assessed on that subset, a cell with zero clean faces (which on `cylinder_3d.msh` turns out to be exactly its ~4896 originally-inverted cells, confirmed empirically) is excluded entirely. `nonOrthoAngleMetric()` itself is untouched (refactored to share its per-face computation via a new exported `faceOrthoAngles()`, confirmed byte-identical output via the milestone-7 gate) — still matches checkMesh's own equally-contaminated value exactly, since checkMesh reads the same raw orientation with no correction (decision #31). A `"pass"`-severity "Faces next to inverted cells excluded" Finding states the excluded FACE count (not cell count — a shared internal face is one exclusion, not two); if every face ends up excluded, the finding is instead "Non-orthogonality can't be assessed until inverted cells are fixed." On `cylinder_3d.msh`: max 80.83°, mean 45.09°, min 20.10°, 744 of 4896 assessable cells ≥70° (warn tier), 0 ≥90° (no fail tier), 15412 faces excluded — these are the numbers this fix's implementation actually produces, verified directly, NOT the figures (max 80.84°, mean 59.58°, 2808 ≥70°) Bhuvanesh separately derived from a different method (correcting inverted cells' orientation mesh-wide and including them, rather than excluding their faces) — Bhuvanesh confirmed using the exclusion-based numbers for the test rather than chasing the other method's figures. Also checked whether skewness needs the same fix: it doesn't — its ratio `dot(S,Cpf)/dot(S,d)` has `S` in both numerator and denominator, so a sign flip from an inverted neighbour cancels out exactly, confirmed empirically on the same mesh (max stays ~0.43–0.63, no contamination) | Bhuvanesh caught this in his own analysis of a real mesh; max/min/mean agreed closely with his independent calculation (confirming the exclusion logic itself is correct) even though the aggregate statistics differ from his separate orientation-correction approach, which is a different operation by design |
